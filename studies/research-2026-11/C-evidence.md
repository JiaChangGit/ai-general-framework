## PART 1 — The two arXiv papers (verified from source)

### 1.1 arXiv 2610.29162 [READ abs + HTML, HTML only first 100k of 114k characters]
- **Title:** Why is AI-generated language so hard to understand?
- **Authors:** Thomas Hikaru Clark, Matias B. Martin, Kyle Mahowald. cs.CL / cs.AI. Submitted 21 Oct 2026. CC BY 4.0.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29162 , https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29162
- **Abstract (paraphrase; full text not reproducible through the tool):** examines psycholinguistic features known to raise human processing difficulty (object relative clauses, long syntactic dependencies, low-frequency n-grams). Verbatim fragment: "AI models use these difficult features more often than paraphrases generated by round-trip translation." All nine frontier / near-frontier models studied use more low-frequency n-grams and more inanimate subjects with animate verbs than human writers, with variation across model families. Hypothesis: human language is optimised for communication, whereas AI text is increasingly optimised (by training on math, code, reasoning) for information density. Proof of concept: fine-tuning a small model under a length penalty moved its language away from human-like patterns.
- **Linguistic features reported:** inanimate subjects with animate verbs; low-frequency (unseen/rare) n-grams; long dependency distances; object relative clauses; stacked nouns (noun-noun compounds; only two models changed significantly under round-trip translation in the essay setting); "that" alternations (in the RL experiment the trained model chose the short, "that"-less form "over 98%" of the time at every length-penalty weight lambda > 0).
- **Models:** Claude Sonnet 5, Opus 5, Opus 5.5, Fable 5; GPT-5.6 Luna/Terra/Sol; Kimi K2.5, GLM 5; Olmo 3 7B (Base/Instruct/Think), Llama 3.1 8B; Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct for the RL experiment.
- **Data:** 120 chat conversations (1,428 replies), 45 coding sessions on five repos, 980 essays (PERSUADE 2.0 prompts). Human baselines: OpenSubtitles (chat), StackExchange (coding), 2,934 PERSUADE student essays; Wikipedia as extra reference. Paraphrase control: round-trip translation via Japanese (NLLB-200 3.3B), Russian pivot and TranslateGemma checked.
- **Numbers available as text:** Opus 5.5 is lower than Opus 5 on "19 of 21 comparisons" (non-overlapping 95% CI in eight). Per-feature rates are only in figures, which the tool could not read, so I cannot give them.
- **Chinese?** NO. The authors "only conduct analyses in English" (Limitations). Chinese was not studied.
- **Limitations (authors):** subset of models, English only, few seeds (10 chat topics, 5 repos), causal claims about frontier models limited because post-training could not be observed, limited compute for post-training experiments.
- Relevance: "AI-hard-to-read" patterns are an information-density/length-optimisation effect; the paper does not itself test whether style prompts fix it.

### 1.2 arXiv 2610.23715 [READ abs + HTML]
- **Title:** The Cognitive Cost of LLM Writing: A Reading-Time Comparison with Human Text
- **Authors:** Kamer Cekini, Elisabetta Biondi, Chiara Boldrini, Andrea Passarella, Marco Conti. cs.CL. Submitted 16 Oct 2026.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23715 , https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23715
- **Abstract (paraphrase):** trains RoBERTa-RT on human eye-tracking data to predict word-level reading times, applies it to 496 paired human articles and LLM rewrites. Verbatim fragment: "LLM-generated rewrites tend to be shorter and have slightly lower predicted total reading times." But per-word predicted reading time is higher; differences track longer, less frequent words, greater lexical diversity and higher contextual predictability.
- **Setup:** rewriting model "GPT-5.6 Terra" (reasoning effort medium), source texts CCAT50 (500 selected, 496 used after four refusals). Predictor trained on OneStop (360 readers), GECO, MECO (English-first-language only), Provo as external test. Surprisal from OPT-125M; frequency via wordfreq.
- **Key numbers (human vs LLM rewrite):** predictor MAE 15.00 ms/word on OneStop test (about 7.8% of mean), 16.09 on Provo (about 8.0%). Total predicted reading time 117.10 s vs 115.58 s (-1.19%). Per-word time 229.91 vs 233.39 ms (+1.57%). Word count -2.66%; mean word length 4.95 vs 5.25 chars (+6.06%); mean word frequency -7.61%; MTLD lexical diversity 103.02 vs 134.67 (+31.83%); mean surprisal 2.952 vs 2.755 (-6.51%). Correlations with change in per-word time: word length rho .588, surprisal .438, MTLD .292, frequency -.405; compression vs per-word time rho -.458 (more shortening, more per-word effort).
- **Linguistic features:** word length, word frequency, lexical diversity, predictability (surprisal). It does NOT report inanimate subjects, dependency length or noun compounds.
- **Chinese?** NO. English only; no Chinese data. (MECO contains other languages but only English-first-language participants were retained.)
- **Limitations (authors):** reading times are predicted, not measured (no human participants read the rewrites); predictor error about 8%, not precise for single texts; one LLM, one prompt, no editing; mostly English news; effects are small (1-2%) though highly significant; paragraph-wise prediction due to 512-token limit.
- Relevance: the "cost" is redistributed, not simply larger: shorter text with denser words. Evidence is a model-based prediction, weak for strong claims.

## PART 2 — Papers and reports

### 2.1 Repository context files (AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md)
**Evaluating AGENTS.md: Are Repository-Level Context Files Helpful for Coding Agents?** Gloaguen, Mündler(-Sasahara), Müller, Raychev, Vechev (ETH Zurich / LogicStar). arXiv 2602.11988, 12 Feb 2026, ICLR 2026 listing. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988 [READ abstract page; details from search summaries].
- Measured: SWE-bench tasks with LLM-generated context files, plus a new set of issues from repos with developer-written context files; several agents/LLMs (per secondary summary: Sonnet-4.5, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.1 mini, Qwen3-30B-coder).
- Quotes: "providing context files does not generally improve task success rates"; "increasing inference cost by over 20% on average"; "repository overviews, although popular and recommended by model providers, are not helpful"; "instructions in the context files are well followed by coding agents".
- Secondary (aiweekly.co, SEARCH-ONLY): LLM-generated files added about 2.45-3.92 steps/task and 20-23% cost; human-written files did somewhat better than generated ones; conclusion that files should describe only minimal requirements.
- Limits: Python, issue-resolution focused; no breakdown of which instruction types help; abstract page gives no percentages for success rate.

**Anthropic Claude Code best practices** (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) [READ, saved long page, searched by keyword]. Vendor guidance, not an experiment, but decisive on design: CLAUDE.md "loads ... into every session, which means every line in it takes a little of Claude's attention on every task"; keep only what Claude cannot work out from code; "If you keep adding emphasis to a CLAUDE.md line and Claude still misses it, that's a sign the line belongs in a hook"; "If something has to happen every time, make it a hook, a deny rule, or a managed setting. CLAUDE.md is for guidelines, and Claude is allowed to decide a guideline doesn't apply."; "keep the enforced set small ... If you ever find yourself at forty of them, most are probably preferences". Also: ask Claude to "Interview me until you could write the spec, then write it to SPEC.md"; "after two failed corrections run /clear and write a better prompt instead of a third"; `/code-review` uses a fresh subagent "that never saw your conversation"; subagents cost tokens and can be slower than doing the task directly; skills are lighter than CLAUDE.md (only name/description always loaded). Note: auto mode is "a classifier model judging each action rather than a hard rule", so "never" rules belong in deny rules or hooks. The `prompt`/`agent` hook types are not deterministic.

**OpenAI "Harness engineering: leveraging Codex in an agent-first world"**, Ryan Lopopolo, 11 Feb 2026, https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/ [SEARCH-ONLY; primary page returned HTTP 403]. Per secondary summaries: AGENTS.md used as a roughly 100-line table of contents pointing to a docs/ directory; enforced architectural constraints and linters; "entropy control"; claimed over one million agent-written lines in five months (unverified by me). Treat the numbers as vendor self-report.

### 2.2 Agent skills benchmarks
**SkillsBench: Benchmarking How Well Agent Skills Work Across Diverse Tasks.** arXiv 2602.12670, Feb 2026 (authors not captured). https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670v1 [READ-PARTIAL, 100k of 159k characters].
- 84 tasks (86 before filtering), 11 domains, 7 agent-model configurations, 7,308 trajectories; conditions: no skills / curated / self-generated.
- Curated skills: +16.2 percentage points average (range +13.6 to +23.3 across configurations); +4.5pp in software engineering up to +51.9pp in healthcare; 16 of 84 tasks negative (worst -39.3pp).
- Self-generated: about -1.3pp; "Self-generated Skills provide no benefit on average."
- Count: 1 skill +17.8pp; 2-3 skills +18.6pp (best); 4+ skills only +5.9pp. Comprehensive skills -2.9pp; "focused Skills with 2-3 modules outperform comprehensive documentation."
- Cheap model + skills can beat bigger model without: Claude Haiku 4.5 11.0% to 27.7% with skills vs Opus 4.5 22.0% without. Codex CLI with GPT-5.2 often ignored skills.
- Limits (authors): skills inject extra context, so no length-matched control; benchmark skills are higher quality than the ecosystem average; self-generated condition ran on 5 of 7 configurations; containerised CLI tasks only.
- Software engineering gain was the smallest (+4.5pp): important for a coding-heavy framework.

### 2.3 Context degradation and tool overload
- **Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance**, Kelly Hong, Anton Troynikov, Jeff Huber (Chroma), 14 Jul 2025, https://trychroma.com/research/context-rot [READ]. 18 LLMs; performance "grows increasingly unreliable as input length grows" even on simple tasks; lower question-needle similarity degrades faster; distractors compound; LongMemEval focused prompt (~300 tokens) beat full prompt (~113k tokens) across model families; shuffling haystack improved results; needle position no notable effect. Limits: authors "do not explain the mechanisms"; tasks deliberately simple; distractor analysis small; Chroma is a retrieval vendor (interest).
- **Effective context engineering for AI agents**, Anthropic Engineering, https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents [READ]. Context is finite; aim for "smallest set of high-signal tokens"; compaction; structured note-taking; sub-agents explore with tens of thousands of tokens but return roughly 1,000-2,000; bloated, overlapping toolsets are a common failure mode.
- **Code execution with MCP**, Anthropic Engineering, https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/code-execution-with-mcp [READ]. Loading MCP servers as code APIs on demand cut one workflow from about 150,000 to 2,000 tokens (98.7%); intermediate results stay out of the model's context; PII can be tokenised so it never passes through the model. Vendor self-report, one example.
- **Inverse Scaling in Test-Time Compute**, arXiv 2507.14417 (Jul 2025; TMLR Dec 2025; ICLR 2026 poster) [SEARCH-ONLY]. Longer reasoning can lower accuracy on some tasks (distraction by irrelevant info, drift to spurious correlations); results model-dependent (Opus 4 inverse, o3 positive on one task).
- Tool count: OSWorld-MCP (arXiv 2510.24563) reports accuracy first rises then drops with more tools for Claude 4 Sonnet [SEARCH-ONLY]. RAG-MCP (arXiv 2505.03275) figures conflict across secondary sources (13.62% to 43.13% vs reverse) [SEARCH-ONLY; do not cite numbers]. Blog thresholds (50/200/740 tools) have no traceable primary source; ignore.
- Lost-in-the-middle (Liu et al., 2023) [FROM-MEMORY, UNVERIFIED]; Chroma found no position effect on its NIAH task, which cuts against over-relying on it.
- Gap: I found no controlled study isolating "long always-loaded instruction file length vs compliance" beyond Gloaguen et al. and vendor advice.

### 2.4 Productivity, code quality, over-engineering
- **METR 2025 RCT** (experienced open-source developers; 19% longer with AI, they expected 20% faster) [cited inside METR's 2026 post, which I READ].
- **We are Changing our Developer Productivity Experiment Design**, Becker, Rush, Cunningham, Rein, Mahamud (METR), 24 Feb 2026, https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/ [READ via fetch summary]. Late-2025 data: returning developers (n=10) 18% speedup (CI -38% to +9%); new developers 4% speedup (CI -15% to +9%); 57 developers, 143 repos, 800+ tasks; pay cut $150 to $50/hr; 30-50% of surveyed developers withheld tasks they did not want to do without AI. Quotes: the data "gives us an unreliable signal of the current productivity effect of AI tools"; "our data is only very weak evidence for the size of this increase"; developers are likely "more sped up from AI tools now". So: no reliable 2026 measurement exists. The METR blog index (read) showed no later productivity post through 5 Nov 2026.
- **GitClear AI Copilot Code Quality 2025** (211M changed lines, 2020-2024) [SEARCH-ONLY, secondary coverage; GitClear page not read]. Copy/paste lines exceeded moved lines in 2024 for the first time; moved/refactored code fell from about 25% (2021) to under 10% (2024); churn 3.3% (2021) to 5.7% (2024); duplicate-block increase reported inconsistently (4x to 8x). Correlational, not causal; DORA 2024 reportedly found a quality improvement, so evidence conflicts.
- **What to Cut? Predicting Unnecessary Methods in Agentic Code Generation**, Watanabe, Shirai, Kashiwa, Iida, arXiv 2602.17091 (MSR '26), https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17091 [READ]. 1,664 merged Python agent PRs (197 projects, AIDev dataset). Of 12,343 added methods 97.4% survived to merge, 2.6% deleted; among the 397 PRs with revisions, 9.9% deleted. Classifier AUC 87.1% but precision 4.9%; GPT-4o AUC 0.621. Limits: deletion window only up to merge; label noise; Python only. Note: this is mild evidence that reviewers rarely remove agent code, not proof of bloat.
- **AI-Generated Smells**, Zhu, Tsantalis, Rigby, arXiv 2605.02741, 4 May 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02741v1 [READ]. LLMs make more Long Method smells than humans (Qwen3-coder 480B zero-shot 11 vs human 1); in multi-agent generation (MetaGPT) architectural smells correlate with code volume (rho 0.94 vs total LOC) and "more detailed prompts did not reduce them" (no prompt-stage effect, p > 0.8). Limits: first-pass output, single framework, 90 problems, one human baseline per problem.
- "Refactoring runaway" claims (over half of agent refactorings unnecessary) come from a blog and I could not find the paper [SEARCH-ONLY, unverified; do not cite].

### 2.5 Multi-agent failures, sub-agents, review
- **Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?** Cemri, Pan, Yang, Agrawal, Chopra, Tiwari, Keutzer, Parameswaran, Klein, Ramchandran, Zaharia, Gonzalez, Stoica. arXiv 2503.13657 (NeurIPS 2025 poster), https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657 [READ abstract]. MAST taxonomy: 14 failure modes in 3 categories (specification/system design, inter-agent misalignment, task verification/termination); MAST-Data 1,600+ annotated traces across 7 frameworks; taxonomy built from 150 traces; "performance gains on popular benchmarks are often minimal". Percentages per mode not read.
- **How we built our multi-agent research system**, Anthropic, https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system [READ]. Opus-4 lead + Sonnet-4 subagents beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal research eval (about 20 early queries); token usage alone explained 80% of BrowseComp variance (95% with tool calls and model); agents about 4x chat tokens, multi-agent about 15x. Not suited when agents share context, tasks are heavily interdependent, or value does not justify cost; "most coding tasks have fewer parallelizable parts than research"; vague subagent instructions caused duplicated work.
- **Failure as a Process: An Anatomy of CLI Coding Agent Trajectories**, Zhao et al. (UCL, Nanjing), arXiv 2607.09510, Jul 2026 [READ via alphaxiv summary]; also relevant to 2.7.
- Cross-model review: I found NO fetched study showing that reviewing with a different model beats same-model review. [SEARCH-ONLY nothing]. Anthropic's doc claims a fresh-context subagent reviewer "isn't swayed by the reasoning that produced the code" (vendor claim, not a measurement). Treat cross-model review as unproven.

### 2.6 Memory, handoff, long-horizon
- **Effective harnesses for long-running agents**, Justin Young, Anthropic, 26 Nov 2025, https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents [READ]. Failure modes: declares victory too early; leaves environment with bugs or undocumented progress; marks features done prematurely; wastes time working out how to run the app. Fixes: initializer agent writes a structured feature list (features start failing, set passing only after end-to-end test); git repo plus progress notes read at session start and updated/committed at end; one feature per session; `init.sh` to start the app. Anecdotal; no numbers.
- Anthropic context-engineering post (2.3): compaction plus structured note-taking outside the window.
- **Advanced Context Engineering for Coding Agents**, Dex Horthy (HumanLayer), 29 Aug 2025, https://www.humanlayer.dev/blog/advanced-context-engineering [READ]: "frequent intentional compaction", utilisation "in the 40%-60% range"; write progress to a markdown file then reload in fresh context. Self-reported, anecdotal, vendor.
- Anthropic memory-tool results [FROM-MEMORY, UNVERIFIED]: I did not locate or read the memory-tool benchmark numbers; do not cite.

### 2.7 Debugging and root cause
- **Failure as a Process** (2607.09510) [READ summary]: 1,794 trajectories, 7 models, 3 scaffolds on Terminal-Bench. Decisive error at median step 7; lock-in at median step 12; first observable signal at median step 16 (about ten steps late); 28% of failures never give an observable signal. Epistemic errors 57.9% of decisive errors, false premises the largest single trigger (30.7%). 92% of successful runs respond to an error signal before lock-in vs 37% of failed runs; 26% of failed runs claim success with fabricated evidence. Implication: validate premises early, require evidence rather than assertion.
- **Debug2Fix**, Garg and Huang, arXiv 2602.18571 (Feb 2026, revised Apr 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18571 [READ abstract]: giving agents an interactive debugger via a subagent gave more than 20% improvement for some models; GPT-5 and Haiku 4.5 matched or exceeded Sonnet 4.5. In review; Java and Python benchmarks only.
- **ORCA-bench** (arXiv 2607.28545): best RCA accuracy 25.3% Medium, 10.0% Hard; hallucination 7-40%; removing source-code access hurt all metrics [SEARCH-ONLY]. TraceCoder (2602.06875), CogniGent (2601.12522), CodeTracer (2604.11641) [SEARCH-ONLY, abstracts only].
- No fetched study directly tests "reproduce first" or "hypothesis log" as interventions; the evidence is indirect (runtime information, early premise checking, verification).

### 2.9 AI-sounding prose, Chinese
- **Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing** (WikiProject AI Cleanup), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing [READ-PARTIAL, 100k of 251k characters]. Signs: overused vocabulary that shifts by model era; avoiding plain copulas ("serves as"); negative parallelism ("not just X, but Y"); rule of three; inflated significance; trailing "-ing" analysis; vague attribution; formulaic "Despite these challenges" endings; Markdown and chatbot residue; citation defects. Reliability warnings: "Humans are notoriously bad at distinguishing human and LLM-generated text"; detectors have non-trivial error rates; "Please do not merely treat these signs as the problems to be fixed." Descriptive, not policy; English only.
- **Do Large Language Models Have an English Accent?** Guo, Conia, Zhou, Li, Potdar, Xiao; arXiv 2410.15956 (Oct 2024, v3 Jul 2025, ACL 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15956 [READ abstract]. Corpus-level metrics for lexical and syntactic naturalness; French and Chinese benchmark shows "a tendency towards English-influenced patterns"; an alignment method improved Chinese naturalness without hurting general benchmarks. No numbers on the abstract page. This is the closest evidence for 歐化-style drift in LLM Chinese; it is a different term for the same phenomenon.
- **Lost in Literalism: How Supervised Training Shapes Translationese in LLMs**, arXiv 2503.04369 (ACL 2025) [SEARCH-ONLY]: more than 40% of GPT-4 English-Chinese translations had translationese errors; 76% for Mistral-7B; over 34% of examined fine-tuning data showed translationese. Also a 2025 English-to-Chinese news study (arXiv 2506.22050) [SEARCH-ONLY]. Both concern translation tasks, not free generation.
- **An Investigation of Translationese in the Generations of Multilingual LLMs**, Valentini, Wright, Granados, Colunga, von der Wense, arXiv 2608.17399, 18 Aug 2026, COLM 2026 [READ abstract]: five languages (not named on the page; whether Chinese is included is unconfirmed); abstract poses the questions without results. Search snippet claims effects "consistently exacerbated" in non-English generation [SEARCH-ONLY].
- **Characterizing Bias: Benchmarking LLMs in Simplified versus Traditional Chinese**, Lyu, Luo, Kang, Koenecke, arXiv 2505.22645 (FAccT 2025) [READ abstract]: 11 models; most favoured Simplified Chinese responses in regional term choice (mainland vs Taiwan names for items); causes suggested: training-data representation, tokenisation. Closest evidence for Taiwan usage drift; it measures choice behaviour, not free-text drift.
- TMMLU+ (arXiv 2403.01858): Traditional-Chinese models trail Simplified counterparts partly due to vocabulary differences (e.g. 內存 vs 記憶體) [SEARCH-ONLY].
- Chinese stylometry: Li and Zhang, CCL 2025, Weibo comments dataset of 463,382, stylometric classifier F1 91.8% for short Chinese comments [SEARCH-ONLY]. Short social-media text only.
- Gap: I found no peer-reviewed study of 歐化中文 specifically in LLM output, nor Simplified-character leakage into Traditional output, nor any validated rule list for "AI-flavoured Chinese". Anything such a framework says about specific Chinese tells is currently expert judgement, not measured.

## PART 4 — Synthesis: ten best-supported findings

Confidence: H = at least one primary source READ, quantitative or multiple agreeing sources; M = primary read but anecdotal/vendor or single study; L = secondary only.

1. **(a) Always-loaded text should be minimal and non-redundant.** Strongest: Gloaguen et al. 2602.11988: context files do not generally raise success and raise cost over 20%; repo overviews are unhelpful; instructions are followed, so unnecessary requirements make tasks harder. Support: Chroma context rot; Anthropic best practices. Confidence M-H (Python issue-fixing only; no study of very long instruction sets).
2. **(a)/(b) Fewer, focused skills beat many or comprehensive ones; self-written skills do not help.** Strongest: SkillsBench: 2-3 skills +18.6pp vs 4+ skills +5.9pp; comprehensive -2.9pp; self-generated -1.3pp; but software engineering gain only +4.5pp. Confidence M (single benchmark, no length-matched control). Implication: skills written from human experience and kept small are plausible; auto-generated ones are not supported.
3. **(b) Skills and CLI/scripts are cheaper than eager MCP definitions; MCP via code execution can cut tokens massively.** Strongest: Anthropic code-execution-with-MCP (150,000 to 2,000 tokens, one example); Willison and Ronacher (8k tokens for one MCP server). Confidence M (vendor/one-example plus practitioner opinion); the "CLI 100% vs MCP 72%" blog claim is SEARCH-ONLY and should not be used.
4. **(c) Cross-session handoff works best with structured, verifiable state files, not free prose.** Strongest: Anthropic long-running harness (feature list that starts failing, progress file, git commits, init script) plus Horthy's compaction-to-file. Confidence M (anecdotal, no measured effect sizes). Cross-model handoff has no direct evidence.
5. **(d) Token cost is driven by context length, tool count and multi-agent fan-out.** Strongest: Anthropic multi-agent post (agents about 4x, multi-agent about 15x chat tokens; tokens explain 80% of BrowseComp variance); Gloaguen (+20% cost from context files). Confidence M-H. Use subagents for isolation of noisy work, not by default: they can even be slower (Anthropic best practices).
6. **(e) Do not expect prompts to prevent bloat; use deterministic checks and scope control.** Strongest: AI-Generated Smells (detailed prompts did not reduce architectural smells; volume correlates rho 0.94); Anthropic: hooks/tests/CI for must-happen rules. Counter-evidence: Watanabe et al. found 97.4% of agent methods survive to merge, so bloat is not always caught in review. GitClear trends are correlational. Confidence M-L.
7. **(f) Debugging failures are mostly early false premises that go undetected; require evidence and runtime information.** Strongest: Failure as a Process (decisive error median step 7, first signal step 16, false premises 30.7%, 26% of failures claim success with fabricated evidence); Debug2Fix (+20% with interactive debugger for some models). Confidence M (benchmarks, not RCA-specific; "reproduce-first" itself untested).
8. **(g)/(h) Asking questions on underspecified tasks helps, but over-asking costs.** Strongest: Ambig-SWE (up to +74%), Ask or Assume? (69.4% vs 61.2%), ClarEval (penalises excess questions). All read only as snippets. Confidence L-M. Vendor guidance endorses an "interview me" spec step. Telling users what is misunderstood is supported only indirectly (false premises as the top failure cause).
9. **(j) Enforceable boundaries must be deterministic (hooks, deny rules, sandbox, CI); instruction text is advisory.** Strongest: Anthropic best practices (READ): "CLAUDE.md is for guidelines, and Claude is allowed to decide a guideline doesn't apply"; classifier-based auto mode is judgement, so "never" rules need deny rules or hooks; prompt/agent hooks are not deterministic; keep the enforced set small. Also MCP privacy: tokenise PII outside model context. Confidence M (vendor documentation, no measured compliance rates).
10. **(i)/(k) Overthinking is real but model-dependent; AI-sounding prose is measurable in English but Chinese-specific evidence is thin.** (i) Inverse Scaling in Test-Time Compute (SEARCH-ONLY): longer reasoning can reduce accuracy on some tasks; model-dependent. (k) Clark et al. 2610.29162 (English only): AI text uses more low-frequency n-grams and inanimate-subject/animate-verb patterns than humans; Cekini et al. 2610.23715 (English only): shorter text, higher predicted per-word effort (+1.57%, small, predicted not measured); Guo et al. (ACL 2025): English-influenced syntax in Chinese output, fixable by alignment (not by prompting alone); Lyu et al.: Simplified-variant preference in regional term choice. Wikipedia warns signs are descriptive and humans detect AI text near chance. Confidence: M for English, L for Chinese (no direct Chinese study of the Part 1 features exists in what I found).

### Evidence gaps to flag for the maintainer
- No controlled evidence on cross-model review, "reproduce first" debugging, hypothesis logs, or long always-on instruction files specifically.
- METR has no reliable 2026 productivity number; do not cite an 18% speedup as established.
- Karpathy, Cherny, Hashimoto, Willison's lethal trifecta, 12-factor agents, lost-in-the-middle, and the Anthropic memory-tool results were not read from primary sources.
- Vendor posts (Anthropic, OpenAI, HumanLayer, Chroma) are self-reported and interested.
- Several figures in fetched summaries came through a small summarising model; re-check any number before publishing it.
